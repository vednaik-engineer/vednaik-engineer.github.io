Role Mechanical Engineer

A tactile 3D function visualizer, a 21×21 grid of pins that physically forms the shape of any surface, built to help students feel the contours of complex 3D math that graphing calculators only show on a flat screen. The finished prototype forms a given surface across the full grid in under 90 seconds and is currently in use in a multivariable calculus class.

Inspiration

The concept traces back to a classic pin-impression toy: push your hand into one side, and thousands of pins hold the impression on the other. VisualMath Pro scales that same idea up to a 21×21 servo-driven grid that can hold any 3D surface, not just a handprint.

Concept

Each of the 21×21 pins raises to a set height, so the grid physically forms a given 3D surface. I developed it with two classmates who led the electrical and software sides, while I handled the mechanical design and build solo.

Build

I designed it in SolidWorks and built it from 3D printed parts and off-the-shelf hardware, on a $400 school-funded budget, as much a test of whether the idea could be built affordably as whether it could be built at all.

The finished prototype forming a 3D surface across the full pin grid

Pin Drive

The pin-pushing mechanism needed to turn a servo's rotation into linear motion, which called for bevel gears, too small to 3D print reliably and too costly to have custom-made across 21 units. I used off-the-shelf LEGO plastic gears instead, which held tight tolerances with minimal backlash at a fraction of the cost.

Drive Array

All 21 gear modules mount together on a single rail, forming the gantry that indexes across the full 21×21 grid, setting each column of pins as it moves through its pass.

Pin Lock

Unlike a pin-impression toy, each pin had to hold its set position rather than settle back under its own weight, while staying easy to push up and easy to reset by hand. I tried several locking designs (leaf-spring and semi-active) before landing on one reliable across all 21 modules without blowing the budget.

I'm now developing a second version to address the first prototype's remaining shortcomings.
