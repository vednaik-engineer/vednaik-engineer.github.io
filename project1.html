MIT Motorsports

Structural Carbon Fiber Seat

I designed, analyzed, and manufactured MIT Motorsports' first fully structural driver's seat, enabling a non-structural firewall and cutting 4 kg from the seat/firewall system on the MY25 car.

Team MIT Motorsports
Year 2025 (MY25)
Role Seat Lead

MIT Motorsports' first fully structural driver's seat, replacing the reliance on a structural firewall with a load-bearing seat design. The result cut 4 kg from the combined seat/firewall system on the MY25 car, a meaningful weight savings on a car where every kilogram affects lap time.

Construction

A carbon fiber sandwich structure with a foam core, engineered to meet FSAE structural requirements while staying as light as possible.

Design & Analysis

I modeled it in Siemens NX and verified deflection and yield margins by hand calculation, using material properties from carbon fiber coupon testing run earlier in the season.

Mold

I CNC-machined a negative mold from foam to form the surface the carbon fiber layup was built against.

Finishing

I finished and sanded the mold surface by hand ahead of layup, since any imperfection would telegraph directly into the final carbon shell.

Layup

I hand-laid a carbon fiber sandwich wet layup over the mold and vacuum bagged it to cure. I also designed and turned a set of aluminum hardpoints that mount through the shell at the load-bearing attachment points, transferring load into the vehicle frame without crushing the foam core.

Driving

My work extended beyond the seat: telemetry data helped me dial in the car's setup ahead of competition. At FSAE Michigan 2025, I drove both Autocross and Endurance, helping bring the team to a 9th place overall finish, the best result in team history since 2017.
