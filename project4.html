David Good Bridge Building Contest – NJIT

3D Printed Bridge

I designed and 3D printed a "locking arch" bridge that set new high school, collegiate, and overall test-bench records at NJIT's David Good Bridge Building Contest, holding over 500 lb on a structure weighing under 500 g.

Competition NJIT, 2023
Result 1st Place – Overall Record
Role Design Team Lead

A "locking arch" bridge built for NJIT's David Good Bridge Building Contest, which judges entries on the ratio of load held to structure weight. The bridge set new high school, collegiate, and overall test-bench records, holding 495 lb on a structure weighing 497.8 g (a roughly 450:1 load-to-weight ratio) for 1st place.

Design

3D printed parts are strong in compression but fail much more easily under tension or interlaminar shear, since FDM layers bond far more weakly across layer lines than within them. I designed the bridge's geometry, a "locking arch," to keep as much of the structure in pure compression as possible under load, always loading the print in the direction it's strongest. Getting there took several redesign passes: I moved from initial sketches through stress analysis and back again each round.

Testing

The bridge was loaded on the NJIT test bench at increments of 15 kgs until the rig could not fit anymore weight. For safety reasons, the bridge could only be tested to a maximum of 225 kgs.

This bridge was built with the help of four other students. The following year, I mentored the next team through the same competition, and they applied the same approach to a 3rd-place finish.
